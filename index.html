Abstract

Scooping lets a robotic hand lift and grasp objects through contact with the supporting surface. This page presents diffusion policy rollouts and the data collection demonstrations used to study this motion with an AIDIN Robotics hand.

Contact is challenging for robotic hands with rigid fingertips. Fingertip force sensing supports compliant interaction during scooping, as illustrated in the principle and comparison videos below.

As an additional technique, we compensate for the hand's low mechanical stiffness by transforming fingertip sensor forces into the wrist frame and adding them to wrist force feedback for the manipulator's compliance controller.

Additional Technique

Compensating for Low Mechanical Stiffness

To compensate for the robotic hand's low mechanical stiffness, we augment wrist force feedback with forces measured by the fingertip sensors. Each fingertip wrench is transformed into the wrist frame and added to the wrist F/T reading, allowing the manipulator to respond compliantly to fingertip contact during scooping.

Wrench Aggregation to Wrist Frame

Each fingertip wrench \(\mathbf{w}_i = (\mathbf{f}_i,\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_i)\) measured in the fingertip frame is transformed to the wrist frame \(\{W\}\) via the kinematic transform \({}^W T_i\). The rotation matrix \(\mathbf{R}_i\) and position offset \(\mathbf{r}_i\) are obtained from TF.

\[ \mathbf{f}_i^W = \mathbf{R}_i \, \mathbf{f}_i \]
\[ \boldsymbol{\tau}_i^W = \mathbf{R}_i \, \boldsymbol{\tau}_i + \mathbf{r}_i \times \mathbf{f}_i^W \]

The aggregated wrench at the wrist combines all \(N\) fingertip contributions and is merged with the physical wrist F/T reading \((\mathbf{f}_\text{wrist},\,\boldsymbol{\tau}_\text{wrist})\):

\[ \mathbf{f}_\text{total} = \mathbf{f}_\text{wrist} + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \mathbf{f}_i^W, \qquad \boldsymbol{\tau}_\text{total} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_\text{wrist} + \sum_{i=1}^{N} \boldsymbol{\tau}_i^W \]

This combined wrench \((\mathbf{f}_\text{total},\, \boldsymbol{\tau}_\text{total})\) is fed as the external force input to the impedance controller, enabling the manipulator to react compliantly to both wrist-level and fingertip-level contacts during the scooping grasping motion.

Fingertip Force Transformation — the fingertip F/T values are transformed and aggregated to the wrist frame for compliance control.

Comparison: w/ vs. w/o Wrist Wrench Aggregator

The hand's low mechanical stiffness can limit the response to fingertip contact when using wrist feedback alone. Adding the transformed fingertip forces to the wrist feedback helps the manipulator respond more sensitively to floor contact.
